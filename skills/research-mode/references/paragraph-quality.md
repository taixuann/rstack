# Paragraph quality

What the critic checks in prose mode, and what the outline questions in Paper writing come from. The critic names the rule and the line. It never writes the better sentence.

## Paper level (Mensh and Kording)

1. One central contribution, and the title states it.
2. Written for a reader who does not know the work: terms defined, no unexplained abbreviation.
3. Context, content, conclusion at every scale: the paper, each section, each paragraph. A paragraph opens with what the reader needs, gives the content, and ends on what it means.
4. No zig-zag: a topic is treated once, in one place. Parallel ideas are written in parallel form.
5. The abstract tells the whole story. The introduction shows the gap and why it matters. The results are a sequence of statements, each supported by a figure, that lead to the contribution. The discussion says how the gap was filled, the limits, and the relevance.

## Sentence level (Gopen and Swan)

1. The verb follows its subject closely. A long interruption between them is a finding.
2. The end of the sentence carries the new information that matters.
3. The start of the sentence names whose story it is, and links back to what the reader already has.
4. A sentence with no link to the one before it is a logical gap.
5. The action is in the verb, not buried in a noun ("we measured", not "measurement was performed").
6. Context comes before the new thing.

## Words

- One term for one thing through the whole paper. A second name for the same thing is a finding.
- A vague quantity word (significant, high, several, considerable) where the report has the number.
- "Significant" used without a test.
- Stacked hedges (may possibly suggest). A claim worded more strongly than its entry in `claims.yaml`.
- An abbreviation used before it is defined, or defined and used once.
- A sentence that carries two messages, or a paragraph that carries two points.
