### Inquiry

State: explore. After the user has read and annotated papers in Zotero. Turn what the user read, and what the assistant read, into a good set of questions, extend them beyond one paper, and send them out to be answered. Talk first; search later.

**Understanding ladder.** Every statement in `docs/wiki/` carries a label and moves up only through review: `[user]` (the user's reading, unverified), `[assistant]` (the assistant's reading, unverified), `[source-checked]` (a critic confirmed it against the paper), `[verified: cite]` (confirmed by an external source that passed a cite-check).

1. **Collect.** Run `scripts/inquiry_extract.py <project>`. It adds new question-like annotations (red highlights and comments with a question mark) to `docs/rstack/explore/inquiry.md` under Open, once each.
2. **Align in your own words.** Per paper and theme, write `docs/wiki/papers/<cite>/alignment.md`: what the user read (`[user]`, from their colours and comments, verbatim) beside what the source analysis says (`[assistant]`). Say it back to the user in your own words and let them correct you.
3. **Interrogate both readings.** Run `interrogate` in interview mode, in short back-and-forth rounds. Test the user's understanding and your own. Classify each item as confirmed, misconception (cite the passage), unknown, or my error. Hand each misconception and unknown to the **learn** skill when the user wants to understand it, not just see it flagged. Use `scientific-critical-thinking` on the assumptions behind each item.
4. **Extend beyond the paper.** Do not stay inside one source. For each theme use `brainstorming` moves (gap, transfer from neighbouring fields, inversion, change of scale or constraint, failure analysis) to add questions the papers do not raise. Pull from the other papers in `references.yaml` too. Mark added questions as proposals.
5. **Write `inquiry.md`.** Group by theme under plain-language headings. Each question: the user's words or the proposed question, where it came from, its class from step 3, and its triage: answered by this paper, answered by another paper, needs search, or for the supervisor. Show it and wait. The user confirms before anything costs time or money.
6. **Send out.** Write the needs-search items to `docs/rstack/explore/inquiry-search.md` with the questions, the context, and the papers already read. Run them only with the user's go-ahead, by `paper-lookup` or `openalex-database` for targeted searches, or by HyperResearch (from `~/academic-space`; save its report to `docs/raw/reports/YYYY-MM-DD_<tool>_<slug>.md`, where every claim stays a lead until a paper that passed review confirms it) or `deep-research` for a broad pass. Results are leads: resolve every DOI, let the user add chosen papers to Zotero, then run **Paper ingestion** on them with `swarm`, one analyst per paper.
7. **Resolve.** When sources return, use `arena` for competing explanations of a mechanism and `interrogate` on the proposed answers. Answer from papers that passed review, with cite and page, and say plainly when the papers do not settle it.
8. **Promote and file.** Put verified material into `docs/wiki/` with `karpathy-llm-wiki`, keeping the ladder labels on every statement.
9. **Claims.** Write `docs/rstack/explore/claims.md`: one line per claim, with branches mechanism, evidence (cite and page), caveat, open question, and a Mermaid mindmap (`markdown-mermaid-writing`). Claims come only from answered items and from statements at `[source-checked]` or above. The user confirms before they feed `03` to `05`.

**Reply:** misconceptions and unknowns found, questions added, items waiting for search or the supervisor, and the claims proposed.
