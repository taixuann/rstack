# Materials and devices: what to ask

The other references in this skill were written for research in general. This page says what the same questions look like for a materials or device study. It lists questions, not thresholds: a required value comes from the venue's rules or the method's standard, never from here.

## The unit of replication

- What is n: devices, samples, batches, measurements, or cycles? They are not the same.
- Do the replicates share a batch, a substrate, a synthesis run or a measurement day? Devices from one batch are not independent of each other. Treating them as independent is pseudoreplication.
- Is the reported spread between devices, between batches, or between repeated measurements of one device? The text must say which.
- Is the best device reported in place of the distribution? Ask for all devices made, how many worked, and the criterion for leaving one out.

## Measurement

- Instrument, model and settings: are they enough for someone to repeat the measurement?
- Calibration: against what, and when?
- Is the number limited by the instrument (range, resolution, noise floor, contact resistance, drift) and not by the sample?
- Environment during the measurement: temperature, humidity, atmosphere, light, time since fabrication.
- Order effects: does measuring change the sample (heating, degradation, hysteresis, conditioning cycles)? Was the order of conditions randomised or at least recorded?

## Controls and comparisons

- Is there a reference sample made and measured the same way, on the same days?
- Is the comparison with the literature made under the same conditions and with the same definition of the metric?
- Is a claimed mechanism shown by an experiment that could have failed, or only consistent with the data?
- What else changed with the variable of interest: thickness, morphology, purity, contact area?

## Reporting

- Every value has a unit, an uncertainty and its kind (SD, SEM, confidence interval), and n.
- Normalised quantities say what they are normalised to (area, mass, volume) and how that was measured.
- Exclusions have a criterion written before the data were seen.
- Stability and lifetime claims give the conditions and the duration actually measured, not an extrapolation.
- Negative and null results that bear on the claim are reported.
- Raw data and analysis code are available, or the paper says why not.
